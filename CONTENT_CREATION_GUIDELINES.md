## Overview

## Part 1: Writing Standards

### 1.1 Tone & Voice

**Safy Tone**:
- **Professional but approachable**: We're not academic. We're practical.
- **Conversational**: As if explaining to a colleague, not a textbook
- **Empathetic**: Acknowledge challenges employees face
- **Optimistic**: Solutions are possible; training helps
- **Action-oriented**: Focus on "what to do," not just "what is"

**Example - ❌ Avoid**:
> "The mitigation of cyber threats necessitates the implementation of comprehensive security protocols..."

**Example - ✅ Preferred**:
> "Here's how to keep your company's data safe: Start with these three simple security habits..."

### 1.2 Reading Level

**Target Audience**: SME employees (mixed education levels)  
**Target Reading Level**: 8th-10th grade (US grade level)

**Guidelines**:
- Average sentence length: 12-15 words (max 25 words)
- Avoid jargon; explain technical terms when first used
- Use short paragraphs (2-4 sentences max)
- Use bullet points for lists (5-7 items max per list)
- Active voice preferred (80%+)

**Readability Tool**: Use Hemingway Editor or Grammarly for all text content

**Example - ❌ Avoid**:
> "The utilization of sophisticated cryptographic algorithms is requisite for the protection of sensitive organizational data assets."

**Example - ✅ Preferred**:
> "Use encryption to protect your company's sensitive information. It's like using a secure lock on your data."

### 1.3 Language & Terminology

**Language Style**:
- Use simple, direct language
- Define technical terms the first time they appear
- Use consistent terminology throughout (create glossary)
- Avoid idioms that don't translate well to other languages

**Terminology Examples**:
- ❌ "Break the bank" → ✅ "Cost too much money"
- ❌ "Piece of cake" → ✅ "Easy to do"
- ❌ "At the end of the day" → ✅ "Finally" or "In summary"

**Regional Terminology**:
- Use locally appropriate terms
- Example: "SME" is the same in Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian
- Example: "CEO" might need explanation in some contexts
- Regional translators will adapt; writers should flag potential issues

### 1.4 Content Structure

**Module Structure**:
```
MODULE (Total: 15-25 minutes)
├─ Lesson 1: Introduction/Overview (3-5 min)
├─ Lesson 2: Core Concept (5-8 min)
├─ Lesson 3: Practical Application (5-8 min)
└─ Lesson 4: Summary & Quiz (2-5 min)
```

**Lesson Structure**:
```
LESSON (5-8 minutes typical)
├─ Opening Hook (10-15 seconds)
│  ├─ Ask a question
│  ├─ Start with a scenario
│  └─ Or state the learning goal
├─ Content Section (4-6 minutes)
│  ├─ Main idea (explained clearly)
│  ├─ 2-3 supporting concepts
│  ├─ Examples or case study
│  └─ Visual breakup
└─ Closing (30-45 seconds)
   ├─ Summarize key takeaway
   ├─ Connect to real work
   └─ Preview next step
```

### 1.5 Accessibility & Inclusion

**Guidelines**:
- Describe visual elements in text (for accessibility)
- Use inclusive language (avoid assumptions)
- Include diverse examples and faces
- Provide transcripts for all video content
- Use clear, high-contrast formatting

**Inclusive Language**:
- ✅ "Team members" (instead of "guys")
- ✅ "They" for unknown gender (instead of "he/she")
- ✅ Show diverse examples (different genders, ages, ethnicities)
- ✅ Use person-first language when discussing disabilities

## Part 2: Content Types & Formats

### 2.1 Video Content

**Video Script Guidelines**:

**Structure**:
- **Hook** (0:00-0:15): Grab attention. Ask question or state value.
- **Content** (0:15-1:45): Deliver main lesson with examples
- **Takeaway** (1:45-2:30): Summarize and call to action

**Script Format**:
```
[SCENE 1: HOOK - 0:00-0:15]

SPEAKER: "Have you ever received an email that looked like it was from your bank, 
but felt a bit off?"

VISUAL: Close-up of suspicious email on screen

[SCENE 2: CONTENT - 0:15-1:45]

SPEAKER: "That's phishing. It's one of the most common cyber attacks. 
Here's why it works and how to spot it."

VISUAL: Animated diagram showing phishing process

[Repeat for each concept...]

[SCENE 3: TAKEAWAY - 1:45-2:30]

SPEAKER: "Remember: Before clicking any link, ask yourself: 
'Did I expect this email? Does it look professional?' 
If not, report it. That's your job."

VISUAL: Key points on screen as text overlay
```

**Video Best Practices**:
- **Pacing**: Deliver 2-3 main ideas per 2-min video
- **Visuals**: Every 10-15 seconds, visual should change
- **Text on Screen**: Use sparingly; support narration
- **Tone**: Professional, conversational, human
- **Length**: 2-3 minutes (target: 2:30)
- **Subtitles**: Always include (for accessibility + mobile viewing)

**Voiceover Guidelines**:
- Speak clearly and naturally (not like a robot)
- Use varied pace and tone (avoid monotone)
- Pause between sentences for processing
- Emphasize key words naturally
- If using multiple speakers, vary voices
- Record in high-quality audio (44.1 kHz, 16-bit minimum)

**Graphics & Animation**:
- Use simple, clean designs
- Consistent colors (use Safy brand colors)
- Avoid overcomplicated animations (distract from message)
- Include captions/text overlays for key points
- Use screenshots for tutorials (highlight the important part)
- Commission 2-3 custom graphics per video (not all stock footage)

**Accessibility**:
- Include subtitles/captions (SRT format)
- Describe visual elements verbally when important
- High contrast for all on-screen text
- Audio description track (optional but recommended)

### 2.2 Interactive Content

**Branching Scenarios** (recommended format):

**Purpose**: Teach decision-making and consequences

**Structure**:
```
Scenario Setup (read by user):
"You're in a meeting. A colleague shares confidential client information 
in a WhatsApp group that includes people from other companies. What do you do?"

Option A: "Report it to your manager"
→ CORRECT: Feedback explaining why this is right
→ Next scenario or lesson

Option B: "Message the colleague privately to ask why they shared"
→ PARTIALLY CORRECT: Feedback explaining it's a start, but manager should know
→ Follow-up scenario

Option C: "Do nothing; they probably have their reasons"
→ INCORRECT: Feedback on why this is risky
→ Educational message on company data policy
→ Retry option or move forward
```

**Interactive Best Practices**:
- 3-4 decision points per scenario (don't overwhelm)
- Clear, unambiguous choices (not trick questions)
- Meaningful consequences (choice should matter)
- Instructional feedback (why was that right/wrong?)
- Tie back to policy or best practice
- No negative tone (even for "wrong" answers)

**Interactive Types to Use**:

1. **Branching Scenario** (60% of interactive content)
   - Decision-making practice
   - Real-work situations
   - Feedback loop

2. **Matching/Sorting** (20% of interactive content)
   - Match concepts to definitions
   - Sort by risk level or category
   - Drag-and-drop format

3. **Hotspot** (10% of interactive content)
   - Click on parts of an image to learn more
   - Anatomy of a policy document
   - Interface walkthrough

4. **Branching Video** (10% of interactive content)
   - User clicks during video to choose path
   - More engaging than linear
   - Requires scripting multiple paths

### 2.3 Text Content (Job Aids, Guides)

**Text Content Structure**:
```
[Module Name] - Quick Reference Guide

OVERVIEW
[1-2 sentence summary of what this is and why it matters]

KEY CONCEPTS
- [Concept 1]: [2-3 sentence explanation]
- [Concept 2]: [2-3 sentence explanation]
- [Concept 3]: [2-3 sentence explanation]

BEST PRACTICES CHECKLIST
☐ [Do this]
☐ [Do this]
☐ [Avoid this]
☐ [Check this]

REGIONAL NOTES
Thailand: [specific note]
Vietnam: [specific note]
Indonesia: [specific note]
Philippines: [specific note]

COMMON MISTAKES
❌ Mistake 1: [What people do wrong and why]
❌ Mistake 2: [What people do wrong and why]

RESOURCES
- [Link to policy]
- [Link to tool]
- [Contact for questions]
```

**Text Guidelines**:
- 1-3 pages maximum (people won't read more)
- Heavy use of formatting (bold, bullets, sections)
- Visual elements (icons, photos)
- Scannable structure (headers, white space)
- Practical focus (not theoretical)
- Include specific examples

### 2.4 Assessment Questions

**Question Quality Standards**:

**Each question should**:
- ✅ Test one specific learning objective
- ✅ Be clear and unambiguous (no trick questions)
- ✅ Have only one correct answer
- ✅ Have plausible (but clearly wrong) distractors
- ✅ Avoid using "all of the above" or "none of the above"
- ✅ Be at appropriate difficulty level
- ✅ Use realistic scenarios when possible

**Question Types**:

**Multiple Choice (40% of questions)**:
```
What is the first step when you suspect phishing?

A) Delete the email immediately
B) Report it to your IT team (CORRECT)
C) Reply asking if it's real
D) Click the link to verify

Feedback: Correct! Your IT team can investigate and protect others 
if it's a phishing attack. Never click suspicious links.
```

**Scenario-Based (40% of questions)**:
```
Your colleague accidentally shared a client's password in a Slack message. 
What should you happen next?

A) Ignore it; it's just a password (INCORRECT)
B) Contact your colleague and inform IT immediately (CORRECT)
C) Change the password yourself
D) Post about it in the team channel

Feedback: Correct! Compromised credentials need immediate action. 
Alert your colleague and IT security without delay.
```

**True/False (20% of questions)** - Use sparingly:
```
True or False: You can open email attachments from unknown senders 
as long as they look like regular files.

CORRECT ANSWER: False
Feedback: Malware can hide in legitimate-looking files. 
Only open attachments you expect from known senders.
```

**Assessment Best Practices**:
- Passing score: 70%+ (standard compliance training)
- 2-3 questions per lesson
- Mix question types
- Provide constructive feedback (not just "correct/incorrect")
- Allow 2 attempts for most assessments
- No time limits (this is learning, not speed testing)

## Part 3: Regional Adaptation

### 3.1 Cultural Considerations

**Thailand**:
- Respect for hierarchy and authority
- Importance of saving face (avoid public criticism)
- Local business customs (wai greeting in videos)
- Formal tone appropriate
- Use Thai examples (Bangkok business culture)
- Thai regulations and compliance standards

**Vietnam**:
- Relationship-focused business culture
- Respect for age and experience
- Direct communication valued
- Use Vietnamese context and examples
- Vietnamese regulatory requirements
- Economic development pace considerations

**Indonesia**:
- Islamic cultural context (appropriate dress, timing)
- Diverse employee base (multiple religions, languages)
- Emphasis on harmony and consensus
- Respect for local diversity
- Indonesian business practices and regulations
- Regional variations within Indonesia

**Philippines**:
- Strong English proficiency (but include translations)
- Relationship-oriented business culture
- Emphasis on family and community
- Filipino examples and context
- Philippine regulatory requirements
- Spanish/English colonial business heritage

### 3.2 Customization Checklist

For each module, customize:
- [ ] **Examples**: Replace generic with region-specific cases
- [ ] **Regulations**: Use regional laws and requirements
- [ ] **Business Practices**: Reflect how business is done locally
- [ ] **Cultural References**: Make content culturally relevant
- [ ] **Tools/Systems**: Reference local tools and platforms
- [ ] **Communication Style**: Match local business communication norms
- [ ] **Visuals**: Include people and settings from the region

**Example - Phishing Content Customization**:

*Generic version*:
> "Phishing emails often claim you need to verify your account."

*Thailand version*:
> "Phishing emails often impersonate Thai banks or payment platforms. 
> They might ask you to verify your account quickly to avoid losing access."

*Vietnam version*:
> "Phishing attacks often target Vietnamese business owners. 
> They may claim to be from government agencies or business partners you know."

### 3.3 Localization vs. Translation

**Do NOT just translate**:
- Generic English content translated directly loses impact
- Regional examples become irrelevant
- Tone may not fit cultural context
- Technical terms may not have direct equivalents

**DO localize**:
- Start with region-specific examples
- Use local case studies
- Reference local regulations
- Adapt tone to cultural norms
- Have regional experts review

**Regional Expert Review Timeline**:
- Week 1-2: Provide drafts to regional managers
- Week 6: SMEs review from regional context
- Week 9-10: Final regional team approval before launch

## Part 4: Quality Assurance

### 4.1 Self-Review Checklist (Writers)

Before submitting content, writers must verify:

**Content Accuracy**:
- [ ] All facts verified (use 2+ sources)
- [ ] No outdated information
- [ ] Complies with current policy
- [ ] Reviewed by SME (informal check)

**Content Clarity**:
- [ ] Hemingway Editor grade: B or higher
- [ ] No jargon unexplained
- [ ] Sentences average <18 words
- [ ] Paragraphs <4 sentences

**Content Engagement**:
- [ ] Opening hook is compelling
- [ ] Examples are concrete and relevant
- [ ] Tone is conversational
- [ ] Conclusion ties back to stated objective

**Content Structure**:
- [ ] Clear lesson organization
- [ ] Logical flow between concepts
- [ ] Each lesson has purpose
- [ ] Fits into larger module narrative

**Formatting**:
- [ ] Consistent formatting throughout
- [ ] Proper headings hierarchy
- [ ] Bullet points used appropriately
- [ ] No spelling or grammar errors

### 4.2 Content Lead Review Checklist

Content Lead reviews for:

**Strategic Alignment**:
- [ ] Aligns with learning objectives
- [ ] Fits within module scope
- [ ] Appropriate depth (not too basic, not too advanced)
- [ ] Regional customization noted

**Quality**:
- [ ] Factual accuracy (spot-check key claims)
- [ ] No sensitive content without approval
- [ ] Appropriate tone for audience
- [ ] No bias or discrimination

**Consistency**:
- [ ] Terminology consistent with other modules
- [ ] Style matches Safy voice
- [ ] Assessment aligned with content
- [ ] Interactive components make sense

**Completeness**:
- [ ] All 4 lessons present
- [ ] All content types included (if applicable)
- [ ] No placeholder text
- [ ] Asset list complete

### 4.3 Revision Workflow

**Content Lead Feedback Format**:
```
SECTION: [Section Name]
ISSUE: [Specific issue]
FEEDBACK: [What to fix]
PRIORITY: High / Medium / Low
ACTION: Rewrite / Clarify / Remove / Verify

Example:
SECTION: "Lesson 2: Cloud Security Basics"
ISSUE: Sentence is too long and complex
FEEDBACK: Break into 2 sentences. Current: 35 words. Target: <25 each
PRIORITY: Medium
ACTION: Rewrite
```

**Revision Response**:
- Writers have 2 business days to revise
- Submit revised version with change tracking visible
- Respond to each feedback point (even if "no change needed")
- Request clarification if feedback unclear

**Re-Review Process**:
- Content Lead checks revisions
- If still issues: Another round (max 2 rounds per section)
- If resolved: Approved and ready for next stage

## Part 5: Brand & Style Guide

### 5.1 Safy Brand Voice

**Three Pillars**:
1. **Empowering**: We help people do their jobs better
2. **Clear**: Complex topics explained simply
3. **Practical**: Focused on what actually works

**Word Choices**:
- ✅ Use: help, guide, learn, practice, protect, build
- ❌ Avoid: problem, difficult, boring, complicated, force

**Tone Examples**:
```
What we sound like:
"Here's a practical way to prevent data loss..."
"Let's walk through the steps together..."
"You've got this. Here's how..."

What we don't sound like:
"It is imperative that you understand..."
"Failure to comply will result in..."
"This is critically important to note..."
```

### 5.2 Visual Standards

**Color Palette** (Use in graphics/interactive):
- Primary: Safy Blue (#0066CC)
- Secondary: Accent Orange (#FF9900)
- Neutral: Light Gray (#F5F5F5)
- Text: Dark Gray (#333333)

**Typography** (for graphics):
- Headings: Sans-serif (Helvetica, Arial, or open-source equivalent)
- Body: Sans-serif, 14-16pt minimum (for readability)

**Image Guidelines**:
- Use diverse people (gender, age, ethnicity, ability)
- Professional but warm tone
- Real situations (not overly staged)
- High quality (1920x1080 minimum)
- Use local stock photos where possible

**Icon Guidelines**:
- Consistent style across all content
- Simple, recognizable (not overcomplicated)
- At least 1 icon per screen/section
- Use for visual scanning (help users find information)

### 5.3 Accessibility Standards

**Text Accessibility**:
- Minimum 14pt font size for all text
- High contrast: Text dark on light (or light on dark)
- Line spacing: 1.5x minimum
- Justified left (not center or right)

**Media Accessibility**:
- All videos include subtitles
- All interactive components keyboard accessible
- Color not the only way to convey information (use symbols too)
- No auto-playing audio
- Provide text alternatives for images

**Cognitive Accessibility**:
- Clear, simple language
- Consistent navigation
- Predictable interactions
- Avoid cognitive overload (not too much at once)
- Provide progress indicators

## Part 6: Compliance & Legal

### 6.1 Content Compliance

**Before Publishing, Verify**:
- [ ] No trademarked terms used incorrectly
- [ ] No company logos without permission
- [ ] No real employee names/photos (unless permission)
- [ ] No discriminatory language or examples
- [ ] Complies with regional content standards
- [ ] GDPR compliant (no personal data in examples)
- [ ] No medical/legal advice (only general information)

### 6.2 Data Privacy

**For Regional Content**:
- Thailand: Comply with PDPA (Personal Data Protection Act)
- Vietnam: Comply with Law on Information Security
- Indonesia: Comply with Law No. 27 of 2022
- Philippines: Comply with Data Privacy Act

**In Practice**:
- Don't use real employee data
- Don't share company-specific policies verbatim
- Use generic examples instead of actual cases
- Anonymize any real scenarios
- Get legal review for sensitive content

### 6.3 Export Considerations

**Regional Restrictions**:
- Content must comply with export laws (OFAC, EAR)
- No military/weapons-related content
- No crypto/blockchain (unless explicitly approved)
- IP and licensing reviewed before deployment

**Check with Legal**:
- Any content involving government/security
- Any content with financial calculations
- Any international transaction examples
- Any content with critical infrastructure references

## Part 7: Common Content Mistakes (Avoid These)

### 7.1 Writing Mistakes

| ❌ MISTAKE | ✅ FIX | WHY |
|-----------|-------|-----|
| Jargon without explanation | Explain term first time used | Audience may not understand |
| 50+ word sentences | Break into 2-3 sentences | Hard to comprehend |
| Negative tone in feedback | Constructive, supportive tone | Impacts learner motivation |
| "Always" / "Never" statements | Use "Usually" or "In most cases" | Exceptions exist |
| Wall of text | Use formatting, bullet points | Hard to read and remember |
| Unclear references | Be specific about what/who | Confusion about context |
| Inconsistent terminology | Define term, use same word always | Confuses learners |

### 7.2 Interactive Mistakes

| ❌ MISTAKE | ✅ FIX | WHY |
|-----------|-------|-----|
| Trick questions | Questions test understanding | Learners get frustrated |
| Unclear choices | Make differences obvious | Users confused about choice |
| No feedback | Explain why answer is correct/wrong | Learners don't learn |
| Too many options | 3-4 choices maximum | Cognitive overload |
| Scenario disconnected from module | Tie back to learning objective | Seems random |
| Punitive feedback | Supportive, instructional tone | Negative impact on learning |

### 7.3 Video Mistakes

| ❌ MISTAKE | ✅ FIX | WHY |
|-----------|-------|-----|
| Robotic, monotone narration | Natural, conversational tone | Boring, disengages viewers |
| No visual changes (static) | New visual every 10-15 sec | People lose attention |
| Long, rambling videos (5+ min) | Target 2:30, max 3:30 | Too long for attention span |
| Cluttered on-screen text | Text supports narration, not duplicates | Too much info at once |
| Poor audio quality | Use professional mic, clean audio | Distraction, hard to understand |
| No subtitles | Include SRT subtitle file | Accessibility, mobile viewing |

## Part 8: Feedback & Approval Workflow

### 8.1 Submission Requirements

Before submitting content to Content Lead:

**All Documents Should Include**:
- [ ] Title and module name
- [ ] Date and writer name
- [ ] Section headings and structure
- [ ] All text in consistent format (Google Docs or markdown)
- [ ] Any external assets referenced (video scripts, graphics list)
- [ ] Self-review checklist completed

**File Naming Convention**:
```
[Category]_[ModuleName]_[ContentType]_[Version].docx

Examples:
- Ethics_BusinessIntegrity_VideoScript_v1.0.docx
- Security_CloudSecurity_TextGuide_v2.1.docx
- Phishing_Advanced_InteractiveSpec_v1.0.docx
```

### 8.2 Review Timeline

**Standard Content Lead Review**:
- Submission: Writer submits by 5 PM
- Review: Content Lead reviews by next 10 AM (next business day)
- Feedback: Provided in shared doc with tracked changes
- Revision: Writer has 2 business days to revise
- Approval: Content Lead approves revised version (48 hours)

**Fast-Track Review** (if critical path):
- Submission: Before 10 AM
- Review: Same day by 4 PM (conditions: pre-reviewed outline + clear submission)
- Revision: Next business day
- Approval: 24 hours after revision

## Part 9: Resource Templates

### 9.1 SME Interview Template

**Scheduled Interview**:
- Date: [Date/Time]
- Duration: 60-90 minutes
- Platform: Zoom/Teams
- Recording: Yes (with permission)
- Attendees: Content Lead, Writer(s), SME, [Optional: Video Producer]

**Interview Agenda**:
1. Intro & Background (5 min) - Why this topic matters
2. Topic Deep Dive (30 min) - Your expertise and experience
3. Real-World Examples (30 min) - Stories, case studies, lessons learned
4. Regional Variations (15 min) - How does this apply in [specific country]?
5. Q&A & Follow-up (10 min) - Clarifications

**Questions to Ask**:
- What's the most common mistake people make?
- What's a real example you've seen?
- How does this apply specifically in [country]?
- What should employees absolutely understand about this?
- What's changed in the last 2 years?
- What's the worst outcome you've seen from not doing this?

### 9.2 Content Revision Template

**Revision Request**:
```
WRITER: [Name]
CONTENT: [Title]
REVISION ROUND: 1 of 2 (max)
DUE DATE: [Date]

FEEDBACK:

Section 1: [Issue]
- [Specific problem]
- [How to fix it]
- [Example of correct approach]

Section 2: [Issue]
- ...

OVERALL: [Praise for what's good + summary of changes needed]
```

**Writer Response**:
```
WRITER: [Name]
REVISION RESPONSE: Round 1

[Topic]: [Action taken]
- [Specific change made + why]

[Topic]: [Action taken]
- [Specific change made + why]

NOTES: [Any questions or concerns from writer]
```
