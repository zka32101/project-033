## 6. RESISTANCE MANAGEMENT

### 6.1 Types of Resistance

**Type 1: Rational Resistance** (Based on facts/logic)
- "I don't see how this improves our current process"
- "The data doesn't support this change"
- **Response**: Provide data, evidence, technical proof, comparative analysis

**Type 2: Emotional Resistance** (Based on feelings/identity)
- "I've done this the old way for 5 years, it works"
- "I'm worried I'll lose my position or value"
- **Response**: Validate feelings, highlight continuity, emphasize value, reassurance

**Type 3: Political Resistance** (Based on power/influence)
- "This undermines my team's authority"
- "I wasn't consulted in this decision"
- **Response**: Involve in decisions, respect autonomy, clarify roles, include in planning

**Type 4: Habitual Resistance** (Based on routine/comfort)
- "I just prefer the way things used to be"
- "Change is uncomfortable, I'll wait it out"
- **Response**: Emphasize benefits, create urgency, make new way easier, patience + reinforcement

### 6.2 Individual Resistance Management

**For Identified Resistors** (6-8 people):

1. **Understand Root Cause** (One-on-one, 30 min)
   - Listen without judgment
   - Ask clarifying questions
   - Identify specific concerns
   - Document concerns

2. **Personalized Support Plan** (Tailored to person)
   - Address specific concerns directly
   - Provide extra training if needed
   - Assign peer mentor if helpful
   - Create clear success criteria
   - Regular check-ins (weekly)

3. **Find Legitimate Value** (Show what's in it for them)
   - Career development opportunity
   - Skill advancement
   - Recognition potential
   - Lighter workload (if true)
   - Team bonding

4. **Escalate if Needed** (After support attempt)
   - Manager conversation (professional development framing)
   - Coaching / organizational development support
   - Clarify expectations (this is not optional)
   - Document support provided
   - Final conversation (compliance required)

### 6.3 Managing Difficult Conversations

**Conversation Framework** (for addressing resistance):

1. **Open** (Set positive tone)
   - "I appreciate your perspective"
   - "I value your experience"
   - "I want to understand your concerns"

2. **Explore** (Understand their perspective)
   - "What specifically concerns you?"
   - "Help me understand how this impacts you"
   - "What would make you feel more confident?"

3. **Respond** (Address their concern directly)
   - Share relevant information/data
   - Clarify misconceptions
   - Acknowledge legitimate concerns
   - Explain benefits

4. **Collaborate** (Find solutions together)
   - "What would help you transition?"
   - "What support would make you confident?"
   - "How can I help you succeed?"

5. **Close** (Clear next steps)
   - Agree on support/timeline
   - Set follow-up check-in
   - End on positive note
   - Emphasize that we're in this together

## 8. RISK SCENARIOS & CONTINGENCY PLANS

### 8.1 High-Risk Scenarios

**Scenario A: Low Adoption (<70% by Oct 31)**
- Warning signs: Training completion <60%, confidence <70%
- Impact: Launch delay, product readiness questioned
- Contingency actions:
  - Immediate feedback collection (what's not working?)
  - Increase support (more office hours, 1-on-1s)
  - Identify and address barriers
  - Extend timeline if absolutely needed
  - Executive visibility and commitment

**Scenario B: Key Staff Departure (1-2 people)**
- Warning signs: Resignation notices, disengagement
- Impact: Knowledge loss, team morale impact
- Contingency actions:
  - Knowledge transfer plan for departing person
  - Cross-train replacement
  - Adjust team structure if needed
  - Maintain morale through communication

**Scenario C: Technical Issues (System not ready)**
- Warning signs: Sandbox environment issues, bugs in production code
- Impact: Confidence drops, training feels irrelevant
- Contingency actions:
  - Transparent communication about status
  - Adjust training to match available features
  - Delay timeline if needed (gate decision)
  - Emphasize learning approach vs. system limitations

**Scenario D: Launch Day Chaos (Many issues, overwhelmed team)**
- Warning signs: Identified during dry-runs
- Impact: Poor user experience, team stress
- Contingency actions:
  - Activate escalation procedures
  - Bring in external support/contractors
  - Implement phased rollout (gradual user activation)
  - 24/7 support team activation
  - Executive support/visibility

### 8.2 Monitoring & Escalation

- Training completion <80% by Sep 25 → Discuss with leads

- Tier 2 pass rate <80% by Oct 31 → Tutoring + timeline review
